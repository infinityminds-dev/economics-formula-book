01 / Statistics for Economics

Formulas grouped by the relevant Statistics topics.

Collection, Organisation & Presentation of Data

Pie Diagram — Central Angle

\[\theta=\frac{\text{Component Value}}{\text{Total Value}}\times 360^\circ\]

Use: To calculate the angle of a sector in a pie diagram.

Class Mark / Midpoint

\[m=\frac{\text{Upper Class Limit}+\text{Lower Class Limit}}{2}\]

Use: To find the midpoint of a class interval, especially for the continuous-series mean.

Measures of Central Tendency

Arithmetic Mean — Individual Series (Direct)

\[\bar X=\frac{\sum X}{N}\]

Symbols: ΣX = sum of observations; N = number of observations.

Use: When individual observations are given.

Arithmetic Mean — Individual Series (Shortcut)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=A+\frac{\sum d}{N}\\ d&=X-A\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: A = assumed mean; d = deviation from A; N = number of observations.

Use: When deviations from an assumed mean make calculation easier.

Arithmetic Mean — Individual Series (Step-Deviation)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=A+\frac{\sum d'}{N}\times C\\ d'&=\frac{X-A}{C}\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: A = assumed mean; C = common factor; d′ = step-deviation.

Use: When values or class intervals share a common factor.

Arithmetic Mean — Discrete Series (Direct)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=\frac{\sum fX}{N}\\N&=\sum f\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: f = frequency; X = value; N = total frequency.

Use: When each value is given with its frequency.

Arithmetic Mean — Discrete Series (Shortcut)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=A+\frac{\sum fd}{N}\\d&=X-A,\quad N=\sum f\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: A = assumed mean; f = frequency; d = X − A.

Arithmetic Mean — Discrete Series (Step-Deviation)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=A+\frac{\sum fd'}{N}\times C\\d'&=\frac{X-A}{C},\quad N=\sum f\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: A = assumed mean; C = common factor; f = frequency.

Arithmetic Mean — Continuous Series (Direct)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=\frac{\sum fm}{N}\\m&=\frac{\text{Upper Limit}+\text{Lower Limit}}{2}\\N&=\sum f\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: f = frequency; m = class mark; N = total frequency.

Arithmetic Mean — Continuous Series (Shortcut)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=A+\frac{\sum fd}{N}\\d&=m-A,\quad N=\sum f\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: A = assumed mean; m = class mark; f = frequency.

Arithmetic Mean — Continuous Series (Step-Deviation)

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=A+\frac{\sum fd'}{N}\times C\\d'&=\frac{m-A}{C},\quad N=\sum f\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: A = assumed mean; C = common factor; m = class mark.

Weighted Arithmetic Mean

\[\bar X_w=\frac{\sum WX}{\sum W}\]

Symbols: W = weight; X = value.

Use: When different items are assigned different weights.

Median — Individual Series (Odd N)

\[\text{Median}=\text{size of the }\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\text{ item}\]

Use: Arrange observations in ascending or descending order first.

Median — Individual Series (Even N)

\[\text{Median}=\frac{\text{size of }\left(\frac N2\right)^{\text{th}}\text{ item}+\text{size of }\left(\frac N2+1\right)^{\text{th}}\text{ item}}{2}\]

Use: Arrange observations first; take the average of the two middle items.

Median — Discrete Series

\[\begin{aligned}\text{Median}&=\text{size of the }\left(\frac{N+1}{2}\right)^{\text{th}}\text{ item}\\N&=\sum f\end{aligned}\]

Use: Find cumulative frequency and locate the median item.

Median — Continuous Series

\[M=L_1+\frac{\frac N2-CF}{f}\times i\]

Symbols: L₁ = lower limit of median class; N = total frequency; CF = cumulative frequency before median class; f = median-class frequency; i = class interval.

Mode — Individual / Discrete Series

\[\text{Mode}=\text{observation with the highest frequency}\]

Use: Identify the value that occurs most often.

Mode — Continuous Series

\[Z=L_1+\frac{f_1-f_0}{2f_1-f_0-f_2}\times i\]

Symbols: L₁ = lower limit of modal class; f₁ = modal-class frequency; f₀ = preceding frequency; f₂ = succeeding frequency; i = class interval.

Empirical Relationship: Mean, Median & Mode

\[\text{Mode}=3\times\text{Median}-2\times\text{Mean}\]

Use: To estimate one measure when the other two are known, where the empirical relationship is applicable.

Correlation

Karl Pearson — Actual Mean Method

\[\begin{aligned}r&=\frac{\sum xy}{\sqrt{\sum x^2\times\sum y^2}}\\x&=X-\bar X,\quad y=Y-\bar Y\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: X̄ and Ȳ = arithmetic means of X and Y; x and y = deviations from their respective means.

Means of the X and Y Series

\[\begin{aligned}\bar X&=\frac{\sum X}{N}\\\bar Y&=\frac{\sum Y}{N}\end{aligned}\]

Use: To calculate the arithmetic means before finding deviations in Karl Pearson's actual-mean method.

Standard Deviations of X and Y

\[\begin{aligned}\sigma_x&=\sqrt{\frac{\sum x^2}{N}}\\\sigma_y&=\sqrt{\frac{\sum y^2}{N}}\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: x = X − X̄; y = Y − Ȳ; N = number of paired observations.

Covariance of X and Y

\[\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\frac{\sum xy}{N}\]

Use: The support material also expresses Pearson's coefficient as r = Cov(X,Y) / (σₓ × σᵧ).

Karl Pearson — Assumed Mean Method

\[r=\frac{N\sum dxdy-(\sum dx)(\sum dy)}{\sqrt{N\sum dx^2-(\sum dx)^2}\,\sqrt{N\sum dy^2-(\sum dy)^2}}\]

Symbols: Aₓ and Aᵧ = assumed means of the X and Y series; N = number of paired observations.

Karl Pearson — Step-Deviation Method

\[\begin{aligned}r&=\frac{N\sum d_x'd_y'-(\sum d_x')(\sum d_y')}{\sqrt{N\sum {d_x'}^2-(\sum d_x')^2}\,\sqrt{N\sum {d_y'}^2-(\sum d_y')^2}}\\d_x'&=\frac{X-A_x}{i_x}\\d_y'&=\frac{Y-A_y}{i_y}\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: Aₓ, Aᵧ = assumed means; iₓ, iᵧ = common factors for the two series.

Karl Pearson — Direct Method

\[r=\frac{N\sum XY-(\sum X)(\sum Y)}{\sqrt{N\sum X^2-(\sum X)^2}\,\sqrt{N\sum Y^2-(\sum Y)^2}}\]

Use: Directly with paired X and Y observations.

Spearman Rank Correlation — No Tied Ranks

\[r_k=1-\frac{6\sum D^2}{N^3-N}\]

Symbols: D = difference between paired ranks; N = number of pairs.

Spearman Rank Correlation — Tied Ranks

\[r_k=1-\frac{6\left[\sum D^2+\frac{\sum(m^3-m)}{12}\right]}{N^3-N}\]

Symbols: m = number of equal observations in each tied group; add one correction term for every tied group. Give tied observations their average rank.

Index Numbers

Simple Aggregative Price Index

\[P_{01}=\frac{\sum P_1}{\sum P_0}\times100\]

Symbols: P₁ = current-year prices; P₀ = base-year prices.

Rate of Inflation

\[\text{Inflation Rate}=\frac{I_1-I_0}{I_0}\times100\]

Symbols: I₁ = current-year price index; I₀ = base-year price index.

02 / Introductory Microeconomics

Formulas grouped by Introduction, Consumer, Producer, and Market topics.

Introduction — Mathematical Tools, PPF & MRT

Slope of a Straight Line

\[\text{Slope}=\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta X}\]

Symbols: ΔY = change in Y; ΔX = change in X.

Equation of an Upward-Sloping Line

\[Y=a+bX\]

Symbols: a = Y-intercept; b = slope; X = independent variable.

Equation of a Downward-Sloping Line

\[Y=a-bX\]

Symbols: a = Y-intercept; b = magnitude of slope.

Marginal Rate of Transformation (MRT)

\[\text{MRT}=\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta X}\]

Use: To express how much of one good is sacrificed to produce an additional unit of the other good.

Consumer's Equilibrium & Demand

Total Utility

\[TU=\sum MU\]

Use: Total utility is the sum of marginal utilities of the units consumed.

Marginal Utility

\[MU=\frac{\Delta TU}{\Delta Q}\]

Symbols: ΔTU = change in total utility; ΔQ = change in quantity.

Budget Set / Budget Constraint

\[P_xQ_x+P_yQ_y\leq M\]

Symbols: Pₓ, Pᵧ = prices; Qₓ, Qᵧ = quantities; M = consumer's income.

Budget Line

\[P_xQ_x+P_yQ_y=M\]

Use: Shows combinations of two goods that use the consumer's entire income.

Consumer's Equilibrium — One Commodity (Cardinal)

\[\frac{MU_x}{P_x}=MU_m\]

Symbols: MUₓ = marginal utility of good X; Pₓ = price of X; MUₘ = marginal utility of money.

Consumer's Equilibrium — Two Commodities (Cardinal)

\[\frac{MU_x}{P_x}=\frac{MU_y}{P_y}=MU_m\]

Use: Along with the condition that marginal utility diminishes.

Consumer's Equilibrium — Indifference Curve

\[MRS_{xy}=\frac{P_x}{P_y}\]

Condition: The budget line is tangent to the indifference curve and MRS is diminishing.

Slope of Budget Line

\[\text{Slope}=-\frac{P_x}{P_y}\]

Symbols: Pₓ and Pᵧ = prices of the two goods.

Slope of Demand Curve

\[\text{Slope}=\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta Q}\]

Symbols: ΔP = change in price; ΔQ = change in quantity demanded.

Price Elasticity of Demand — Percentage Method

\[E^d=\frac{\%\text{ change in }Q^d}{\%\text{ change in }P}\]

Use: Measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price. The coefficient is commonly reported by magnitude.

Price Elasticity of Demand — Proportionate Method

\[\begin{aligned}E^d&=\frac{\Delta Q}{Q}\times\frac{P}{\Delta P}\\&=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P}\times\frac{P}{Q}\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: P and Q = original price and quantity; ΔP and ΔQ = their changes.

Total Expenditure

\[TE=P\times Q\]

Use: In the total-expenditure method, compare TE before and after the price change to identify elasticity.

Producer Behaviour & Supply

Production Function

\[Q=f(L,K)\]

Symbols: Q = output; L = labour; K = capital. In the short run, at least one factor is fixed.

Average Product (AP)

\[AP=\frac{TP}{L}\]

Symbols: TP = total product; L = units of variable factor (labour).

Marginal Product (MP)

\[\begin{aligned}MP&=\frac{\Delta TP}{\Delta L}\\MP_n&=TP_n-TP_{n-1}\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: ΔTP = change in total product; ΔL = change in labour input.

Total Cost (TC)

\[TC=TFC+TVC\]

Symbols: TFC = total fixed cost; TVC = total variable cost.

Total Fixed Cost (TFC)

\[\begin{aligned}TFC&=TC-TVC\\&=AFC\times Q\end{aligned}\]

Total Variable Cost (TVC)

\[\begin{aligned}TVC&=TC-TFC\\&=AVC\times Q\end{aligned}\]

Average Cost (AC)

\[\begin{aligned}AC&=\frac{TC}{Q}\\&=AFC+AVC\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: Q = output.

Average Fixed Cost (AFC)

\[\begin{aligned}AFC&=\frac{TFC}{Q}\\&=AC-AVC\end{aligned}\]

Average Variable Cost (AVC)

\[\begin{aligned}AVC&=\frac{TVC}{Q}\\&=AC-AFC\end{aligned}\]

Marginal Cost (MC)

\[\begin{aligned}MC&=\frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q}\\&=\frac{\Delta TVC}{\Delta Q}\\MC_n&=TC_n-TC_{n-1}\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: ΔTC = change in total cost; ΔQ = change in output.

Total Revenue (TR)

\[\begin{aligned}TR&=P\times Q\\&=AR\times Q\\&=\sum MR\end{aligned}\]

Average Revenue (AR)

\[AR=\frac{TR}{Q}=P\]

Symbols: TR = total revenue; Q = quantity sold; P = price.

Marginal Revenue (MR)

\[\begin{aligned}MR&=\frac{\Delta TR}{\Delta Q}\\MR_n&=TR_n-TR_{n-1}\end{aligned}\]

Producer's Equilibrium

\[\begin{aligned}MR&=MC\\&\text{MC must cut MR from below}\end{aligned}\]

Use: The equality alone is not enough; the MC curve should be rising and cross MR from below.

Slope of Supply Curve

\[\text{Slope}=\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta Q_s}\]

Symbols: ΔP = change in price; ΔQₛ = change in quantity supplied.

Price Elasticity of Supply — Percentage Method

\[E_s=\frac{\%\text{ change in }Q_s}{\%\text{ change in }P}\]

Price Elasticity of Supply — Proportionate Method

\[\begin{aligned}E_s&=\frac{\Delta Q_s}{Q_s}\times\frac{P}{\Delta P}\\&=\frac{\Delta Q_s}{\Delta P}\times\frac{P}{Q_s}\end{aligned}\]

Symbols: P and Qₛ = original price and quantity supplied; ΔP and ΔQₛ = their changes.

Forms of Market & Price Determination

Market Equilibrium

\[Q^d=Q^s\]

Symbols: Qᵈ = market quantity demanded; Qˢ = market quantity supplied.

Excess Demand

\[\text{Excess Demand}=Q^d-Q^s\]

Use: When quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied at the given price.

Excess Supply

\[\text{Excess Supply}=Q^s-Q^d\]

Use: When quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded at the given price.
